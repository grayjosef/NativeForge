### Structural fact that determines the whole redesign

The SC demo page is **95 sibling `<section>` elements** under one `<main>`:

- 94 of 95 carry the identical class `nf-sc-demo-section`
- 86 contain a `data-testid="sc-demo-*-flags"` machine-flag paragraph
- the section class currently has **no card styling whatsoever** — no border, no
  background, no padding, no elevation. Only `h2 { margin }` and a `.nf-sc-demo-why` colour.

That uniformity is the leverage: the majority of the visual redesign can be delivered
through the shared class and a `[data-testid$="-flags"]` attribute selector, without
rewriting 3780 lines of pinned JSX.

## 3. Current visual weaknesses

Ranked by damage to buyer trust.

1. **A failure banner is the first thing a buyer sees.** The workspace connectivity probe
   renders "We couldn't reach the workspace service. Check your connection or start the
   local API." directly under the header **on the SC customer demo route**. The SC demo is
   a deliberately offline static bridge, so this error is structurally guaranteed to show
   during the demo. It is the single worst item on the page.
2. **Nothing is a card.** 95 sections render as flat headings and grey paragraphs on a flat
   beige field. No borders, no elevation, no containers, no columns. The product reads as a
   printed compliance memo, not as software.
3. **Raw machine key=value dumps are the primary content.** The hero's fourth paragraph is
   `demo_dev_only=true offline_only=true live_ingestion=false source_activation=false
   auth_required=false final_eligibility_claim_allowed=false data_mode=curated_current
   live_ingest_claimed=false automated_refresh_claimed=false pack_id=...`, styled as ordinary
   body text at ordinary body weight. 86 more of these follow. The information is correct and
   must be retained — the failure is that it is presented at the same visual rank as the
   product story, with no framing that says "this is machine-verifiable evidence".
4. **Internal engineering campaign vocabulary is on the customer surface.** `gate10`,
   `gate13`, `gate20/21/32/33/34/35`, `Mode B`, `SCA Gate 16`, "3000-sprint closeout",
   `sca_passed_claimed`. A tribal board member reads this as somebody else's CI dashboard.
5. **Operator chrome is shown to the buyer.** The global header exposes
   Workspace / Workbench / Activation / NM-WA Demo / SC Demo, a Demo|Real plane toggle,
   an Offline pill and a Trust pill — internal switches, on a customer route.
6. **The presenter's talk-track is printed on screen.** `sc-demo-buyer-talk-track` renders
   "Talk-track: limited external / Dev-domain demo of an…" for the audience to read. Speaker
   notes belong to the speaker.
7. **The product headline is an internal artifact name.** `<h1>` is
   "SC Customer Demo — Curated State + Federal Opportunities". The company name and the
   product's actual promise appear nowhere in the hero.
8. **Flat, undifferentiated palette.** Warm beige page, one 1px-2px shadow token, one radius,
   no elevation scale, no type scale, no spacing scale. Accent colours exist
   (`--nf-trust` copper, `--nf-primary` teal) but are barely deployed.
9. **Dead horizontal space.** Content column is ~1040px inside a 1440px viewport with 400px
   of unused right-hand field, while the page is 78,000px tall. The layout wastes the one
   axis it has and abuses the other.
10. **Generic OS font.** `"Segoe UI", system-ui` is the default-internal-tool signal.
11. **Broken token reference.** `.nf-sc-demo-cockpit` uses
    `color-mix(in srgb, var(--nf-bg, #0b0f14) 92%, #1a2a3a)`. `--nf-bg` **does not exist** in
    the stylesheet, so this silently falls back to a near-black `#0b0f14` mix on a light-beige
    page — a leftover from an abandoned palette.

## 4. Current copy weaknesses

- `<title>` is **"NativeForge — M0 demo shell"**. That is the browser tab, the bookmark and
  the screen-share title during the demo.
- Hero stacks six consecutive undifferentiated grey paragraphs: advisory banner, trust strip,
  claim boundary, talk-track, machine flags, NOFO honesty line. All the same weight, all the
  same colour, no ranking.
- The claim boundary — the most credibility-positive content on the page — is styled with
  `.nf-muted`, i.e. **de-emphasised**. Honesty is the product's differentiator and it is set
  in the lightest grey on the page.
- Section headings mix buyer language ("Eligibility and recognition") with internal build
  language ("Gate 34 closeout", "Mode B rehearsal") in one undifferentiated list.
- The `Owner Action Required: Provide real OIDC_* OOB, storage approval/config, and pen-test
  report` line and the `freeze allowed=6 forbidden=12 fake_green=false` line repeat on
  **every** trust view card — the same two strings, ten-plus times, at full body weight.

## 5. Current hierarchy weaknesses

- One flat level. 95 peers, no grouping, no priority, no landmarks.
- The seven surfaces a buyer actually needs (opportunity intelligence, eligibility,
  authority, evidence binder, feedback, sovereignty/trust, next action) are scattered
  arbitrarily among ~85 engineering closeout sections rather than lifted to the top.
- `h2` never changes size across breakpoints, so the heading level carries no weight signal.
- No in-page navigation of any kind for a 78,000px document.

- The **substance** of the claim boundary: allowed vs forbidden claims are genuinely
  well-modelled and honest. This is a real asset that is being visually wasted.
- Recognition-tier handling (federal vs state-only) is correctly distinguished in the data.
- `Owner Action Required` next-action modelling is the right idea.
- Evidence/provenance fields (capture_date, pack_id, source labels) exist and are truthful —
  they need presentation, not invention.

## 8. Biggest buyer-trust risks

1. Connectivity error banner on the demo route — reads as "the product is broken".
2. Machine flag dumps as primary content — reads as "this is a prototype, not a product".
3. Internal gate/sprint/Mode-B vocabulary — reads as "we are watching someone's build log".
4. 78,000px flat scroll — reads as "nobody has designed this".
5. Talk-track printed on screen — reads as "we are being handled".
6. Clipped review table (below) — reads as "the data is broken".

## 9. Mobile / responsive risks

- **Header segmented control is clipped at 390px.** `.nf-segment` measures right edge 504px
  against a 390px viewport; "NM/WA Demo" is cut mid-word with no scroll affordance and no
  way to reach the last controls.
- `h1` and `h2` are both 24px at 390px — no hierarchy at all.
- Hero consumes ~850px before any product content.
- 139,675px page height at 390px.
- No breakpoint-specific density anywhere in the SC stylesheet.

## 10. Functional defect found during the audit

**The review table is clipped and unreachable.** Measured at 1440x1000:

- table width **1751px**
- container (`.nf-sc-demo-section`) width **1040px**
- container `overflow-x: visible`, with an ancestor `.nf-app { overflow-x: clip }`

711px of table — including the right-hand columns — is cut off and cannot be reached by
scrolling in any direction. This is data loss in the UI, not a cosmetic issue. It also
occurs at 1280, 834 and 390.

## 11. Claim-boundary risks (must be preserved through the redesign)

The redesign is constrained by a real, enforced contract. It must not be broken:

- `scripts/verify_nativeforge_demo_deployment.sh` → `claim_boundary_preserved()` scans the
  served **home** HTML for `FORBIDDEN_HTML_CLAIMS`: "controlled customer pilot GO",
  "production rollout GO", "login live", "pen-test passed", "production-ready",
  "production storage", "customer persistence".
- It also requires exactly **one** `nativeforge-build-sha` meta and a
  `frontend/dist/build-manifest.json`.
- The Playwright smoke pins **~90 sections visible** and **~86 `*-flags` elements containing
  exact key=value substrings** (`live_ingest_claimed=false`, `final_eligibility_claimed=false`,
  `monday=GO`, `production=NO_GO`, …).

Consequence for design: the machine flags **cannot be removed and cannot be collapsed into a
closed `<details>`**, because Playwright `toBeVisible()` would fail. They must stay rendered
and visible. The correct move is therefore not deletion but **rank**: keep every flag,
present it as a deliberate machine-verifiable evidence strip that is visually subordinate to
a human-readable statement of the same fact. Playwright's `toBeVisible()` does not require
in-viewport, so relocating dense evidence below the buyer surface is safe.

`sc-demo-buyer-talk-track` is asserted only as `html).toContain("sc-demo-buyer-talk-track")`
— presence in markup, not visibility — so the presenter note may be moved into a collapsed
disclosure.

## 12. Audit conclusion

The product is not failing on substance. The evidence model, the claim boundary and the
honesty posture are genuinely differentiated and are the reason this can be sold to tribal
grant offices and boards. They are failing on **rank and presentation**: correct, valuable
information is being rendered at uniform weight in a 78,000px flat list, with an error banner
on top and a build-log vocabulary throughout.

The redesign therefore does not need to invent anything or hide anything. It needs to:

1. Stop the demo route from showing an infrastructure error.
2. Give the buyer surface a real executive hero and lift the seven buyer-critical sections above the engineering ledger.
3. Turn 95 flat sections into a dense, carded, two-column executive console.
4. Give machine flags a designed evidence treatment instead of body-text treatment.
5. Fix the clipped table and the clipped mobile header.
6. Replace the flat beige token set with a real executive design system (navy/charcoal/ivory/copper, type scale, spacing scale, elevation scale).
