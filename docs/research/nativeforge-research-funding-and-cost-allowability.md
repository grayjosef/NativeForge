# NativeForge Source Registry Research: Research Funding + Cost Allowability

**Research date:** 2026-08-26. **Method:** direct primary-source fetches only (no web search, no mirrors, no archives). eCFR text retrieved from ecfr.gov, "Displaying title 2, up to date as of 8/24/2026. Title 2 was last amended 8/17/2026."

**Honesty posture.** Every eligibility rule, rate, threshold, and quote below was read on a page actually fetched. Items marked **UNKNOWN** or **UNVERIFIED** were not confirmable and must not be presented to customers as fact. Two structural warnings apply throughout:

1. **Agency web content is frequently stale.** Verified instances: `cdc.gov/tribal/` serving 2016 Zika content; `acf.hhs.gov/ana/about` serving 2018 content naming a former Commissioner; IMLS Native program pages advertising FY2025 deadlines while FY26 NOFOs are posted elsewhere on the same site; NEH Preservation Assistance page showing 2018 body text with empty date fields; `fema.gov/grants/mitigation` stamped January 2023. **Never infer "program is open" from "page exists."**
2. **Program volatility is high.** Of nine NSF targets, three are archived or renamed and one could not be located at all. Store stable keys (NSF `pims_id`, solicitation number, Grants.gov opportunity ID, Assistance Listing number), never URL slugs alone.

# PART A — Research Funding Sources

## NSF

**Monitoring architecture (best of the three science agencies).** Two RSS feeds verified working:
- `https://www.nsf.gov/rss/rss_www_funding_pgm_annc_inf.xml` — `application/rss+xml`, 15 items, each linking `/{slug}/nsf26-XXX/solicitation` with "Document Number: NSF 26-526" in the description. **Primary discovery feed.**
- `https://www.nsf.gov/rss/rss_www_funding_upcoming.xml` — carries deadline *type* (Deadline / Target / Window) plus date. **Caveat: 30-day horizon only** — insufficient as a sole source.

`https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json?keyword=tribal` is **verified working** JSON, docs at `resources.research.gov/common/webapi/awardapisearch-v1.htm`. It is an **awards** API — competitive intelligence, not opportunity monitoring. **No NSF funding-opportunity API exists** (no evidence found). `https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities` returns HTTP 200 with an **empty body** (client-side rendered) and is unusable by a plain fetcher.

**robots.txt is materially restrictive.** `Disallow: /funding/opportunities?*`, `/funding/opps`, `/funding/opps?*`, `/funding/opportunities/csvexport*`, `/awardsearch/*`, `/pubs/*`, `/search/`. The faceted search, its query strings, and the CSV export are all off-limits to a compliant crawler. Compliant design = RSS for discovery + direct `/funding/opportunities/<slug>` fetches (allowed) + `api.nsf.gov` for awards.

## CDC

**CDC delegates opportunity listing entirely to Grants.gov.** Operative quote from the CDC Eligibility page: "The eligibility criteria for these awards are found in CDC's Funding Opportunity Announcements (FOAs) listed on www.grants.gov/search-grants." The `cdc.gov/grants` and `/funding` sections are process documentation only — no opportunity inventory, no dates, no FONs.

**Healthy Tribes is the real CDC tribal vehicle — VERIFIED CURRENT** (`https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-tribes/about/index.html`, last updated May 12, 2026). Eligibility quote: "provides funding to American Indian tribes, Alaska Native villages, tribal organizations, Urban Indian Organizations, and Tribal Epidemiology Centers (TECs) through three key cooperative agreements." **Direct applicant, no university intermediary, and notably includes Urban Indian Organizations and TECs.** Three agreements: **GHWIC**, **TPWIC**, **TECPHI**.

**GHWIC — VERIFIED CURRENT** (last updated May 11, 2026), described as "CDC's largest investment" in AI/AN tribal health. Quote: "It reaches over 115 federally recognized tribes and Urban Indian Organizations, either through direct funding or indirect funding through tribal organizations" — **both direct-award and pass-through paths exist.** Research/evaluation angle is explicit: the coordinating center "Establish[es] and lead[s] a national evaluation plan for GHWIC." UIOs enter via a separate non-recognition-based definition ("nonprofit corporate bodies … governed by a board of directors of whom at least 51% are AI/AN adults"). State-recognized: **UNKNOWN**. Next competition / FON / cycle: **UNKNOWN — do not guess.**

**Historical index, not a live feed:** `https://www.cdc.gov/tribal-health/cooperative-agreements/index.html` is stale (last updated May 16, 2024) but names **CDC-RFA-TO-23-0001** "Strengthening Public Health Systems and Services in Indian Country," a 5-year agreement emphasizing "workforce, **data and information systems**, and programs and services." That data-systems emphasis is a documented federal precedent worth pulling for the Part B analysis.

**CDC/ATSDR Tribal Advisory Committee is a leading indicator** — `https://www.cdc.gov/tribal-health/advisory/index.html`, last updated **August 3, 2026** (freshest CDC tribal page found). 34 members: 1 delegate + 1 alternate per each of the 12 IHS Areas, plus 5 National At-Large pairs. Charter revised April 24, 2026. Roster PDFs follow a dated, scrapeable path pattern `/media/pdfs/YYYY/MM/`. TAC agendas and follow-up reports precede Grants.gov postings by weeks-to-months — low-cost, high-signal.

**Legacy trap: blocklist `/tribal/*`, use `/tribal-health/*`.** `cdc.gov/tribal/index.html` still resolves but serves stale legacy content whose most recent substantive item is a **July 2016** Zika letter.

**⚠️ CDC IS THE BIGGEST TERMS RISK IN THIS ENTIRE RESEARCH PASS.** `https://www.cdc.gov/rss` is not a feed index — it returns CDC's syndication Usage Guidelines, which state: **"Redistribution of CDC syndicated content is not allowed"** and **"Per Federal statute (42 U.S.C. 1320b-10), it is not permitted, for a fee, to reproduce, reprint or redistribute CDC content unless expressly granted and documented by CDC."** A paid grant-intelligence product that reproduces CDC page text verbatim sits squarely in the zone this language addresses. Note this constrains *redistribution*, not *reading*. Mitigations to put to counsel: store facts and metadata (FON, dates, eligibility determinations, links) rather than CDC prose; link out rather than embed; or pursue the sanctioned syndication channel at `tools.cdc.gov/medialibrary` (unverified), which would come with explicit terms. **Flagging, not adjudicating — get counsel on 42 U.S.C. 1320b-10 before shipping CDC text.** CDC robots.txt itself is permissive and disallows nothing tribal-, grants-, or funding-related; the constraint is the terms, not robots.

**CDC tribal *research* funding specifically: UNKNOWN, not "none."** CDC confirms it funds research but routes all of it through Grants.gov FOAs.

## HRSA

**The best-structured target in the entire report and the lowest-effort win.** `https://www.hrsa.gov/grants/find-funding` is Drupal 11 **server-rendered — results are in the initial payload**, unlike NIH's SPA. Facets: Keywords; Opportunity Status (Open/Closed/Awarded); Bureau/Office; Sort by (Deadline, Alphabetical, Most Relevant, **Recently Updated, Recently Added**). The "Recently Added" sort is exactly what an incremental scraper wants.

Per-result fields inline on the *listing* page: title, **Funding Opportunity Number**, **Application Deadline** (MM/DD/YYYY), Bureau/Office, Status, Apply link, detail link `/grants/find-funding/{FON}`, **and a full "Who can apply:" eligibility paragraph.** You do not need to fetch detail pages for eligibility.

At fetch time: "1-2 of 2 Funding Opportunities" open agency-wide (`HRSA-27-099`, deadline 09/09/2026, restricted to existing H80 Health Center Program recipients; `HRSA-27-098` MSE, deadline 09/01/2026, accredited medical schools in specified shortage states). **Neither is tribal-relevant.** Useful calibration: HRSA's open-opportunity volume is very low at any moment, so polling is cheap.

**⭐ THE STANDOUT ELIGIBILITY FINDING OF THIS ENTIRE PASS.** `https://www.hrsa.gov/grants/apply-for-a-grant/who-can-apply` (reviewed November 2024), under "What kinds of organizations may apply? → Government," states verbatim: **"Native American tribal (whether the U.S. government recognizes your organization or not)"**.

This is **the only place across NIH, CDC, HRSA, and ANA where an affirmative statement was found that non-federally-recognized tribal organizations may apply.** It contrasts sharply with NIH NARCH/TIRBEE/TURTLE, which are explicitly federally-recognized-only. So: direct applicant **yes**; federal recognition **not required**; state-recognized tribes **qualify** on the plain reading.

**Critical caveat to encode:** this is HRSA's *agency-general* posture. The same page says "Every Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO) details who can apply." Per-NOFO eligibility governs and can be far narrower — as both currently-open HRSA NOFOs demonstrate. **Model this as a default that each NOFO overrides; never let the platform infer per-opportunity tribal eligibility from this page.**

**HRSA tribal-health *research* funding: UNKNOWN / not found.** HRSA's bureau structure is service- and workforce-oriented rather than investigator-initiated. The Federal Office of Rural Health Policy is the most plausible home for tribal evaluation work — **unverified.**

**RSS/API: no evidence found** on either page fetched. GovDelivery email only. robots.txt is standard Drupal; `/grants/find-funding` is **not** under the disallowed `/search/` and is therefore permitted.

**⭐ Eligibility — DIRECT, and tribal colleges are EXPLICITLY EXCLUDED.** Verbatim from the agency-wide eligibility page: "For the Native American Library and Museum Services grant programs, Indian tribes and Alaska Native villages and corporations are eligible to apply for funding. **Entities such as libraries, museums, schools, tribal colleges, or departments of education are not eligible applicants**, although they may be involved in the administration of this program and their staff may serve as project directors." This is the exact inverse of NIFA's model and a high-value distinction for the platform.

NAB: "your Tribe must be **Federally recognized** and offer library and/or archival services to the community." NAE cites the statute: "IMLS adheres to the criteria in our statute which utilizes the defined term 'Indian Tribe'. **20 U.S.C. § 9161**" … "which is recognized as eligible for the special programs and services provided by the United States to Indians" (§ 9101(5)), explicitly including Alaska Native villages, regional corporations, and village corporations. Partnering allowed "so long as the Tribe is the lead applicant." **Federal recognition REQUIRED; state-recognized-only tribes not eligible.**

## Cross-cutting structural findings to encode in the data model

1. **The dominant recall risk is not plumbing, it is classification.** No channel has a "Native-specific" flag beyond coarse eligibility codes. Set-asides and tribal-preference language live in free text and in the `99` / `ET11010` / `ET12010` "unrestricted / determined at NOFO level" buckets. **Filter on codes `07`, `11`, `08` AND `99`, then run a text classifier over Description + Additional Information on Eligibility (4,000 chars) for Tribe/Tribal/Indian/Alaska Native/Native Hawaiian/TCU.** Code-only filtering will systematically miss the largest tribally-eligible population.
2. **"Federally recognized" is the near-universal gate, with exactly one verified exception.** Required at: NSF TCUP and AISL, IMLS NAB/NAE, NEA GAP, NIH NARCH/TIRBEE/TURTLE, USGS consultation, NIFA (via the statutory 1994 list). **The single verified exception is HRSA's agency-general posture: "whether the U.S. government recognizes your organization or not."** Default state-recognized to UNKNOWN/likely-ineligible and flag for human review, with HRSA as the documented counter-example.
3. **Three structurally different applicant models — conflating them produces wrong advice.** (a) *Tribe-as-applicant:* IMLS NAB/NAE (tribal colleges **explicitly ineligible**), NEA GAP, NIH NARCH, CDC Healthy Tribes. (b) *Institution-as-applicant:* all NIFA tribal programs, NSF TCUP, NASA MUREP — a Tribe cannot apply. (c) *Partner/subaward only:* NIH RCMI, USGS CRU and CASC, NIFA FRTEP/NBTS. Tag every record with which model applies.
4. **Native Hawaiian programs run on a separate statutory track** (20 U.S.C. § 7517(2) per IMLS NHLS) and are open to **nonprofits serving Native Hawaiians**, not to a government. Do not fold them into tribal eligibility logic.
5. **Multi-phase and multi-part applications need first-class modeling.** NIFA TCRGP runs Phase II and Phase III deadlines off one NOFO, discoverable only in a program-page events block. NEA GAP requires two submissions on two systems a week apart. Single-deadline models drop both.
6. **Non-.gov downstream is where much tribal-accessible money lives.** Sea Grant's national office had zero open opportunities while linking 34 consortium programs running their own RFPs on `.edu`/`.org` domains — one verified open with tribal eligibility. Same shape at NEA via state arts agencies. **Grants.gov-only ingestion has a systematic blind spot here.**

# PART B — Cost Allowability for Software, Data Systems, and Administrative/Planning Capacity

## B.0 The headline answer

**A grant-management / grant-intelligence platform is very likely payable with federal grant funds — and the 2024 Uniform Guidance revision (89 FR 30136, Apr. 22, 2024) is the reason.** The revision inserted express, on-point language into two sections that did not previously carry it. The two strongest citations in the entire corpus are:

- **2 CFR 200.455(c)** — "**The costs related to data and evaluation are allowable.**" Data costs expressly "include … expenditures needed to gather, store, track, manage, analyze, disaggregate, secure, share, publish, or otherwise use data to administer or improve the program, **such as data systems, personnel, data dashboards, cybersecurity, and related items.**"
- **2 CFR 200.413(b)** — costs "that otherwise would be treated as indirect costs may also be considered direct costs if they are directly related to a specific award (including, for example, extraordinary utility consumption, the cost of materials supplied from stock or services rendered by specialized facilities, **cybersecurity, integrated data systems, asset management systems, performance management costs, program evaluation costs**, or other institutional service operations)."

**What this changes.** Before 2024 the argument for charging a grants-management platform rested on inference from "materials and supplies" and general allocability. It now rests on OMB naming data systems, dashboards, cybersecurity, asset management systems, and performance management as allowable — and, in §200.413(b), as **direct**-chargeable when tied to a specific award. That is a materially stronger position than the market generally assumes.

**What it does not change.** Three limits survive and must be stated to every customer:
1. **Allocability still governs.** A platform serving *all* of a Tribe's grants is not "directly related to a specific award" for any one of them. That pushes it toward the **indirect cost pool** — which is fine, and arguably better, but it is a different mechanic with a different recovery ceiling.
2. **Program-level activity lists still bind.** Uniform Guidance says a cost *type* is allowable; a NOFO says what *activities* the money buys. DOJ CTAS demonstrates both directions at once — it expressly allows case-management data systems and expressly forbids grant-writing consultants.
3. **The "general costs of government" limitation is a live risk for Tribes specifically.** See §B.6.

**One caveat on currency.** The eCFR pages read for this analysis display as "up to date as of 8/24/2026," and Title 2 shows "last amended 8/17/2026." That amendment was **not diffed** against the text quoted here. Re-verify §§200.413, 200.414, 200.455 before relying on them in a client deliverable.

## B.1 2 CFR 200.1 — Definitions (the SaaS classification question)

**URL:** `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-A/subject-group-ECFR2a6a0087862fd2c/section-200.1` · **Source:** 89 FR 30136, Apr. 22, 2024, as amended at 89 FR 79732, Oct. 1, 2024.

**Operative language (short quotes):**
- **Equipment** — "tangible personal property (including information technology systems)" with useful life over one year and per-unit acquisition cost at or above "the lesser of the capitalization level established by the recipient … or **$10,000**."
- **Supply** — "all tangible personal property other than those described in the *equipment* definition." Critically: "A computing device is a supply if the acquisition cost is below the lesser of the capitalization level … or **$10,000**, regardless of the length of its useful life."
- **Information technology systems** — "computing devices, ancillary equipment, **software**, firmware, and related procedures, **services (including support services)**, and resources."
- **General purpose equipment** — "equipment that is not limited to research, medical, scientific, or other technical activities. Examples include office equipment … **information technology equipment and systems** …"
- **Capital expenditures** — "expenditures to acquire capital assets or expenditures to make additions, improvements … that materially increase their value or useful life."
- **Capital assets** — includes "intellectual property (including software)"; **but (2): "capital assets do not include intangible right-to-use assets (per GASB) and right-to-use operating lease assets (per FASB)."**
- **Acquisition cost** — "Acquisition costs for software include those development costs capitalized in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP)."
- **⭐ Intangible property** — "property having no physical existence, such as … data (including data licenses), websites, IP licenses … software, or **software subscriptions or licenses**."
- **Indian Tribe** — "recognized as eligible for the special programs and services provided by the United States to Indians because of their status as Indians. See 25 U.S.C. 5304(e)," referencing the annually published BIA list. **Federal recognition is the definitional test throughout 2 CFR 200.**
- **MTDC** (relevant to §200.414) — "all direct salaries and wages, applicable fringe benefits, materials and supplies, services, travel, and up to the first $50,000 of each subaward … **MTDC excludes equipment, capital expenditures … rental costs** …"

**⚠️ Two textual corrections to the original brief:** §200.1 defines **"Supply"** (singular) — there is **no "Supplies" entry**; and it defines **"Indian Tribe"** with a capital T only.

**Is a SaaS subscription equipment, supplies, or services?** The regulation does not answer this in one place, but three provisions converge and the answer is defensible:
- SaaS is **not tangible personal property**, so it is neither "equipment" nor "supply" on the plain text — both definitions are limited to *tangible* personal property.
- SaaS is expressly **intangible property** — §200.1 names "software subscriptions or licenses" in that definition.
- SaaS is **not a capital asset**, because §200.1 *Capital assets* (2) carves out right-to-use and right-to-use operating lease assets.

**⭐ The practical consequence, and it is favorable: a SaaS subscription is best characterized as a service / intangible right-to-use cost, NOT as equipment and NOT as a capital expenditure. That means §200.439's prior-approval requirements do not attach.** This is the single most useful classification conclusion in Part B. It is an inference from three definitions read together, not a sentence OMB wrote — so present it as a well-supported reading, not as settled law, and expect some awarding officials to disagree.

**Corollary trap:** a **perpetual license that GAAP requires you to capitalize** flips the analysis entirely — it becomes a capital asset ("intellectual property (including software)") with capitalized development costs in its acquisition cost, and §200.439 prior approval attaches. **Subscription pricing is regulatorily cleaner than perpetual licensing.**

**Classification: likely-allowable** (as a service / intangible right-to-use cost, escaping the equipment and capital-expenditure regimes).

## B.2 2 CFR 200.413 — Direct costs

**URL:** `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFRd93f2a98b1f6455/section-200.413`

**Operative language:**
- (b) — costs otherwise indirect "may also be considered direct costs if they are directly related to a specific award," expressly listing "**cybersecurity, integrated data systems, asset management systems, performance management costs, program evaluation costs**."
- (a) — "Costs incurred for the same purpose in like circumstances must be treated consistently as direct or indirect costs."
- (c) *Administrative and clerical staff salaries* — "should normally be treated as **indirect** costs. Direct charging … may be appropriate **only if** they meet **all** of the following conditions: (1) The administrative or clerical services are **integral** to a Federal award; (2) Individuals involved can be **specifically identified** with a Federal award; and (3) The costs are **not also recovered as indirect costs**."
- (d) *Minor items* — "A direct cost of a minor amount may be treated as an indirect cost, for reasons of practicality."

**Analysis.** §200.413(b) is the strongest *direct-charge* authority available for NativeForge-type software, and it is new in the 2024 revision. But note precisely what it says: these costs may be direct **if directly related to a specific award**. A platform bought to manage a Tribe's entire grant portfolio is, by construction, not specific to one award — so the honest reading is that **enterprise-wide deployment belongs in the indirect pool, while a project-specific instance (a compliance or evaluation data system stood up for one award) can be direct-charged under §200.413(b).** Both routes are available; they are not interchangeable, and §200.413(c)(3) and §200.414(f) both forbid recovering the same cost twice.

§200.413(c) is the governing rule for the **administrative capacity/staff** line in the summary table: a grants-administration staffer is presumed indirect, and direct-charging requires satisfying all three conditions — a real documentation burden, not a formality.

**Classification: likely-allowable** — clearly-allowable as a cost *type*, but the direct-vs-indirect placement is fact-dependent, so "clearly" would overstate it.

## B.3 2 CFR 200.414 — Indirect costs, and the de minimis rate

**URL:** `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFRd93f2a98b1f6455/section-200.414`

**⭐ The current de minimis rate is 15 percent of modified total direct costs — verified verbatim from primary source.** §200.414(f): "Recipients and subrecipients that do not have a current Federal negotiated indirect cost rate (including provisional rate) may elect to charge a de minimis rate of up to **15 percent of modified total direct costs (MTDC)**." **This is the 2024 revision — it was 10 percent under the prior text. Any material still saying 10% is out of date.**

Further verified language in (f): "The recipient or subrecipient is authorized to determine the appropriate rate up to this limit." · "Federal agencies and pass-through entities **may not require** recipients and subrecipients to use a de minimis rate lower than the negotiated … rate." · "costs must be **consistently charged** as either direct or indirect costs and **may not be double charged**." · "The de minimis rate **does not require documentation** to justify its use and **may be used indefinitely**." · "Once elected, the recipient or subrecipient **must use the de minimis rate for all Federal awards** until [it] chooses to receive a negotiated rate."

Also verified and directly useful to tribal customers:
- (c)(1) — "Negotiated indirect cost rates **must be accepted by all Federal agencies**," with deviations only per Federal statute or an approved (c)(3) policy; (c)(3) requires agencies to "make publicly available" their deviation criteria; (c)(4) requires the NOFO to state indirect cost policies.
- (d) — pass-through entities "**must accept all federally negotiated indirect costs rates for subrecipients**."
- (e)(5) — **Appendix VII to Part 200 is "States and Local Government and Indian Tribe Indirect Cost Proposals"** — the tribal-specific rate-proposal authority.
- (g) — a **one-time extension of a negotiated rate for up to four years** is available, subject to cognizant-agency approval; no rate review may be requested during the extension.

**⭐ The indirect route is the most durable answer for an enterprise-wide platform**, because it sidesteps every individual program's activity-eligibility list. **⚠️ But quantify it honestly for customers:** at the 15% de minimis rate applied to MTDC, recovery may be far less than the platform costs, and **MTDC excludes equipment, capital expenditures, rental costs, and the portion of each subaward over $50,000** — so the base is smaller than gross award value. A negotiated rate (via IBC, §B.11) will usually recover more, and the "once elected, use for all awards" lock-in in (f) is a real strategic decision, not a checkbox.

**Classification: likely-allowable** via the indirect pool, with recovery capped by the rate and base.

## B.4 2 CFR 200.439 — Equipment and other capital expenditures

**URL:** `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFRed1f39f9b3d4e72/section-200.439`

**Operative language:**
- (b)(1) — "Capital expenditures for **general purpose equipment**, buildings, and land are allowable as direct costs, **but only with the prior written approval** of the Federal agency or pass-through entity."
- (b)(2) — "Capital expenditures for **special purpose equipment** are allowable as direct costs, provided that items with a unit cost of **$10,000 or more** have the prior written approval …"
- (b)(7) — "**Equipment and other capital expenditures are unallowable as indirect costs.**"

**⚠️ This is the single most dangerous trap for a software purchase, and the asymmetry is counterintuitive.** §200.1 defines *general purpose equipment* to include "information technology equipment and systems." Under (b)(1), general purpose equipment requires prior written approval **with no dollar threshold at all** — the $10,000 threshold in (b)(2) applies only to *special purpose* equipment. **So a capitalized IT system, at any dollar value, needs prior written approval as a direct charge.** Practitioners routinely and wrongly assume the $10,000 threshold applies to IT.

**This is precisely why the B.1 classification matters.** If the platform is a **subscription** (intangible right-to-use, not a capital asset, not tangible personal property), §200.439 never engages. If it is **capitalized software**, (b)(1) engages with no de minimis floor, and (b)(7) additionally forbids putting it in the indirect pool. **Structure as a subscription.**

**Classification for a capitalized software purchase: sometimes-allowable-depends-on-NOFO** — allowable, but conditioned on prior written approval that the awarding agency may decline.

## B.5 2 CFR 200.453 / 200.314 / 200.313 — Supplies, computing devices, equipment stewardship

**URLs:** `.../subject-group-ECFRed1f39f9b3d4e72/section-200.453`; `.../subpart-D/subject-group-ECFR8feb98c2e3e5ad2/section-200.314`; `.../section-200.313`

**§200.453 operative language:** (a) "Costs incurred for materials, supplies, and fabricated parts necessary for the performance of a Federal award are allowable." (c) "**Charging computing devices as direct costs is allowable for devices that are essential and allocable, but not solely dedicated, to the performance of a Federal award.**"

§200.453(c) is genuinely helpful and often overlooked: a computing device need **not be solely dedicated** to one award to be direct-charged — only "essential and allocable." By analogy that supports shared-use IT costs generally, though the section speaks to devices rather than services.

**§200.314 (Supplies) traps:** residual **unused** supplies exceeding **$10,000** in aggregate at the end of the period of performance trigger federal compensation rights; "Unused supplies means supplies that are in new condition, not having been used or opened before"; and "the recipient or subrecipient **must not use supplies acquired with the Federal award to provide services for a fee that is less than a private company would charge**." Aggregate value "consists of all supply types, not just like-item supplies."

**§200.313 (Equipment) traps** — these are the reason to avoid the equipment characterization entirely: conditional title vesting; property records including FAIN, title holder, acquisition date, cost, federal contribution percentage, location, use, condition, disposition; **physical inventory reconciled "at least once every two years"**; a loss/damage/theft control system; disposition at **$10,000** fair-market-value thresholds with the federal agency "entitled to an amount calculated by multiplying the percentage of the Federal agency's contribution … by the current market value." Notably, **§200.313(b): "Indian Tribes must use, manage, and dispose of equipment acquired under a Federal award in accordance with tribal laws and procedures. If such laws and procedures do not exist, Indian Tribes must follow the guidance in this section."** — a real deference provision worth surfacing to tribal customers.

**Classification (SaaS as supplies): unclear.** The tangibility limit in the §200.1 *Supply* definition makes the supplies characterization a poor fit for SaaS despite its intuitive appeal. Cite §200.455(c) and §200.413(b) instead — they are on point and stronger. Classification for **computing devices/hardware: clearly-allowable** per §200.453(a)+(c).

## B.6 ⚠️ 2 CFR 200.444 — General costs of government (the tribal-specific limitation)

**URL:** `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFRed1f39f9b3d4e72/section-200.444`

**Operative language:** (a) "For states, local governments, and **Indian Tribes**, the general costs of government are **unallowable** except as provided in § 200.475." Enumerated unallowable: (1) "Salaries and expenses of … the chief executive of an Indian Tribe"; (2) "Salaries and other expenses of a State legislature, **tribal council**, or similar local governmental body … whether incurred for purposes of legislation or executive direction"; (5) "Costs of other general types of government services normally provided to the general public."

**⭐ (b) — the tribal-favorable safe harbor, quoted:** "**Indian Tribes** and Councils of Governments (COGs) … may include **up to 50 percent of salaries and expenses directly attributable to managing and operating Federal programs by the chief executive and their staff in the indirect cost calculation without documentation.**"

**Why this section matters more than any other for a tribe-wide platform.** §200.444(a) is the textual basis for the objection a reviewer or auditor will actually raise: *a platform that manages the Tribe's whole grant portfolio is an expense of general tribal government, not a cost of any program.* This is not hypothetical — the same reasoning appears independently in **45 CFR 1336.50(f)** (ANA), which allows chief-executive-office costs only where not "a cost of general government" and only if "related to a project assisted under this part."

**The counter-argument, and it is a good one.** §200.444(b) expressly contemplates that **managing and operating Federal programs** is a distinct and recoverable activity, separate from general governance — and lets Tribes book up to 50% of executive-office salaries and expenses into the indirect pool **without documentation**. A grants-management platform is a tool for exactly the activity (b) blesses. **Frame the platform as federal-program management infrastructure, never as tribal administrative modernization.** The framing is not cosmetic; it is the difference between (a) and (b).

**Classification: likely-allowable** via §200.444(b)'s indirect-cost treatment, **unlikely-allowable** if presented as a general tribal government expense.

## B.7 2 CFR 200.460 — Proposal costs (is grant-writing allowable?)

**URL:** `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFRed1f39f9b3d4e72/section-200.460`

**Full operative text (the section is a single paragraph):** "Proposal costs are the costs of preparing bids, proposals, or applications on potential Federal and non-Federal awards or projects, **including developing data necessary to support the recipient's or subrecipient's bids or proposals**. Proposal costs of the current accounting period of both successful and unsuccessful bids and proposals **normally should be treated as indirect costs** and allocated to all current activities of the recipient or subrecipient. **No proposal costs of past accounting periods may be allocated to the current period.**"

**Direct answer to the brief's question: grant-proposal-writing cost is ALLOWABLE — but "normally" as an INDIRECT cost, not as a direct charge.** The section contains no prohibition; it is a *classification* rule. Three consequences:

1. **⭐ "including developing data necessary to support the recipient's or subrecipient's bids or proposals" is the most on-point Uniform Guidance phrase for a grant-*intelligence* platform specifically.** Opportunity discovery, eligibility screening, and competitive/award-history analysis are literally "developing data necessary to support … bids or proposals." Read with §200.455(c) ("data systems… allowable") this is a strong pairing.
2. **"Normally should be treated as indirect" is a presumption, not an absolute** — but a direct charge runs against the text and needs affirmative program authority. That authority does exist in at least one place: **24 CFR 1003.206(d)** (ICDBG) expressly permits it.
3. **The last sentence is an under-appreciated trap:** "No proposal costs of past accounting periods may be allocated to the current period." Proposal-related costs must be booked in the period incurred — you cannot capitalize a year of unsuccessful pursuit and recover it later.

**⚠️ And the countervailing authority must be presented alongside it.** DOJ COPS lists "**Contractor/consultant expenses for grant writing purposes**" as **unallowable** under CTAS Purpose Area 1. Presenting §200.460 without this would be misleading: Uniform Guidance permits proposal costs as indirect; a specific program can and does forbid direct grant-writing procurement.

**Classification: likely-allowable as an indirect cost; unlikely-allowable as a direct charge** absent express program authority (ICDBG being the verified exception).

## B.8 2 CFR 200.455 — Organization costs (⭐ the strongest single citation)

**URL:** `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFRed1f39f9b3d4e72/section-200.455`

**Operative language:**
- (a) — organization/reorganization costs (incorporation fees, promoters, management consultants, attorneys, accountants) "are **unallowable except with prior approval** of the Federal agency."
- (b) — union-organizing persuasion costs "are unallowable."
- **⭐ (c) — "The costs related to data and evaluation are allowable.** Data costs include (but are not limited to) the expenditures needed to gather, store, track, manage, analyze, disaggregate, secure, share, publish, or otherwise use data to administer or improve the program, **such as data systems, personnel, data dashboards, cybersecurity, and related items.** Data costs may also include direct or indirect costs associated with building **integrated data systems** … Evaluation costs include (but are not limited to) **evidence reviews, evaluation planning and feasibility assessment, conducting evaluations, sharing evaluation results**, and other personnel or materials costs related to the effective building and use of evidence and evaluation for program design, administration, or improvement."

**This is the best authority in the corpus for NativeForge and it should lead every client-facing summary.** Note the specific features that make it strong: it is an affirmative allowance ("are allowable"), not a conditional one; the list is expressly non-exhaustive ("include but are not limited to"); it names **data systems**, **dashboards**, and **cybersecurity** as such; it explicitly permits **either** direct **or** indirect treatment for integrated data systems; and its purpose clause — "to **administer** or improve the program" — covers administration, not only research. It also independently supports the **needs assessment** and **feasibility study** lines in the summary table via "evaluation planning and feasibility assessment."

**⚠️ Two honest limits.** (1) The placement is odd — a broad data/evaluation allowance sitting inside a section titled "Organization costs" — which means some awarding officials will not know it is there; cite it by full text, not by section title. (2) One phrase narrows part of it: integrated data systems are described as linking "individual-level data from multiple **State and local government** agencies." Tribes are not named in that clause. The general allowance in the first two sentences carries no such limitation, so the tribal case rests on those; **do not lean on the "integrated data systems" sentence for a tribal customer.**

**Classification: clearly-allowable** for data systems, dashboards, cybersecurity, and evaluation costs. This is the only **clearly-allowable** rating assigned to a Uniform Guidance provision in this analysis, and it is assigned because the text is affirmative, express, and names the exact cost objects.

## B.9 2 CFR 200.430 — Compensation, and 200.475 — Travel

**§200.430** (`.../section-200.430`) — (a) compensation is allowable where total compensation "(1) Is reasonable for the services rendered and conforms to the established written policy of the recipient … consistently applied to both Federal and non-Federal activities." (g)(1) charges "must be based on records that accurately reflect the work performed," supported by internal control, incorporated into official records, and "not exceeding 100 percent of compensated activities." (g)(1)(vii) "Budget estimates … alone do not qualify as support for charges to Federal awards."

**⭐ Tribal-specific flexibility, §200.430(g)(5):** "**States, local governments, and Indian Tribes may use substitute processes or systems for allocating salaries and wages** to Federal awards either in place of or in addition to the records described in paragraph (g)(1) **if approved by the cognizant agency for indirect cost**," including "random moment sampling, 'rolling' time studies, case counts, or other quantifiable measures." (g)(6)–(7) further allow outcome/milestone-based alternatives and blended-funding performance plans with advance approval from all involved agencies. **This is a genuine selling point for a platform that produces defensible effort and allocation records — it is the documentation regime §200.430(g) demands.**

## B.10 2 CFR 200.458 — Pre-award costs, and 200.465 — Rental costs

**§200.458 (full text, single paragraph):** "Pre-award costs are those incurred before the start date of the Federal award or subaward directly pursuant to the negotiation and in anticipation of the Federal award where such costs are necessary for efficient and timely performance of the scope of work. These costs are allowable **only** to the extent that they would have been allowed if incurred after the start date **and only with the written approval of the Federal agency.** If approved, these costs must be charged to the initial budget period."

**⚠️ This is the #1 practical trap for a software vendor.** A Tribe that subscribes in March to help win an award that starts in October has incurred a pre-award cost requiring **written agency approval** — and agency approval is discretionary. Compare **40 CFR 35.513** (EPA), which is more generous but blunt: EPA "may reimburse recipients for pre-award costs incurred from the beginning" of the budget period, but "**The applicant incurs pre-award costs at its own risk**," and they "must be identified in the grant application EPA approves." **Sales guidance: never tell a prospect that a pre-award subscription will be reimbursed. Position the first period as the customer's own-funds investment, or time the subscription to the period of performance.**

**§200.465 — the SaaS-as-rental analogy.** (a) rental costs allowable where rates are reasonable against comparable properties, market conditions, and alternatives, and "Rental arrangements should be reviewed periodically." **⭐ (e) — "Rental or lease payments are allowable under lease contracts where the recipient … is required to recognize an intangible right-to-use lease asset under GASB standards or right-of-use operating lease asset under FASB standards."** This dovetails exactly with the §200.1 *Capital assets* (2) carve-out and is the second pillar of the B.1 conclusion. (b)–(d) limit sale-leaseback, less-than-arm's-length leases (with a detailed family-member list), and finance-lease/financed-purchase treatment to what ownership would have cost. (f) rental of property owned by affiliated individuals "for purposes such as the home office is unallowable."

**Classification (pre-award subscription): sometimes-allowable-depends-on-NOFO** — allowable only with written agency approval. **Classification (SaaS as a right-to-use operating lease cost): likely-allowable** per §200.465(e).

## B.14 Cross-program patterns

1. **⭐ 2024 changed the answer.** The pre-2024 case for a grants platform was inferential. The post-2024 case is textual: §200.455(c) affirmatively allows "data systems, personnel, data dashboards, cybersecurity"; §200.413(b) names "cybersecurity, integrated data systems, asset management systems, performance management costs, program evaluation costs" as direct-chargeable. **Lead with these two.**
2. **Nobody names SaaS at the program level; everybody names "administrative management."** Across every program document read, not one uses the words SaaS, cloud, or subscription. The words that *do* appear: "overall program and/or administrative management" (HUD), "indirect costs, administrative expenses" (FEMA), "Developing systems for assuring compliance with program requirements" (HUD ICDBG), "Data management systems for record keeping and case management" (DOJ). **Frame the product as administrative/compliance-systems cost, never as a "software subscription."**
3. **Two structurally different doors, and the second is better for enterprise deployment.** (a) *Program administrative allowance* — HUD 20%/30% and combined 20%, FEMA 3.34%/4.89% lock-ins: bounded, and competing with staff salaries for the same pot. (b) *Negotiated indirect cost rate via IBC* — unbounded by any program's activity list, and corroborated by DOJ as the expected route. **Door (b) is the durable answer.**
4. **Planning and capacity building are near-universally fundable; grant-seeking almost never is at the program level.** Every program funds planning (EPA §35.545, HUD §1003.205, BIA Category 1, FEMA Part 11, DOE TA, DOJ PA2). **Only 24 CFR 1003.206(d) affirmatively authorizes preparing applications for other federal programs — and DOJ COPS affirmatively forbids grant-writing consultants.** Both must appear in any client-facing summary; presenting one without the other is misleading.
5. **Explicit software allowability tracks program *purpose*, not administration.** DOJ allows RMS, CAD, crime-mapping, and case-management systems because those *are* the funded program. A grant-management platform is an overhead tool and gets judged as overhead — which is exactly why §200.455(c) and the indirect route matter more than any program's equipment list.
6. **The "cost of general government" objection is the recurring tribal-specific risk.** 2 CFR 200.444(a) and 45 CFR 1336.50(f) independently separate tribe-wide governance costs from project costs. A platform serving *all* of a Tribe's grants is the textbook target. **The answer is §200.444(b)'s 50% federal-program-management safe harbor plus the indirect pool — not a direct charge.**

## B.15 The recurring traps (state every one of these to customers)

| Trap | Authority | Practical effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-award costs** | 2 CFR 200.458; 40 CFR 35.513 | Requires **written agency approval**; EPA says "at its own risk." **Never promise reimbursement for a pre-award subscription.** |
| **Capital expenditure prior approval** | 2 CFR 200.439(b)(1) | **General purpose equipment — which includes "information technology equipment and systems" — needs prior written approval at ANY dollar value.** The $10,000 threshold in (b)(2) applies only to *special* purpose equipment. Widely misunderstood. |
| **Capitalized software flips the analysis** | §200.1 *Capital assets*; §200.439(b)(7) | A perpetual license capitalized under GAAP becomes a capital asset, triggering prior approval **and** becoming unallowable as an indirect cost. **Subscription > perpetual license.** |
| **Period-of-performance limits on subscriptions** | EPA 40 CFR 35.548(c) (4 yrs); DOJ COPS (36-month pay-in-full); FEMA 44 CFR 207.8(b) (8 yrs / 2 yrs) | **No program read permits charging a subscription beyond its period of performance.** DOJ additionally bars "service agreements, or prepaid voice and data plans." **Structure as annual, in-period charges.** |
| **Double-dipping** | 2 CFR 200.413(c)(3), 200.414(f); 44 CFR 207.6(b)-(c) | One platform cannot be both an indirect cost and a line item on multiple awards. FEMA is explicit both directions. |
| **Combined caps** | 24 CFR 1003.206 | ICDBG's 20% covers planning **plus** administration together — budgeting the platform under "administration" while separately planning under §1003.205 breaches the cap. |
| **Supplanting** | 34 U.S.C. 10384(a) (DOJ PA1 only, and uniquely **vs. BIA funds** for tribes); ANA maintenance of effort, 45 CFR 1336.50(c); EPA CAA §105, 40 CFR 35.575(b); EPA GAP no-reduction, §35.548(d) | Uneven — **check program by program.** Not found for HUD IHBG/ICDBG, FEMA Part 207, BIA TCR, DOE, NTIA. **Exposure if the Tribe was already paying for the tool from its own funds.** |
| **Match consumption** | 45 CFR 1336.50(b)(1) | ANA's 20% non-federal match means charging a subscription there also consumes match capacity. |
| **Proposal costs cannot be carried forward** | 2 CFR 200.460 | "No proposal costs of past accounting periods may be allocated to the current period." |
| **De minimis lock-in** | 2 CFR 200.414(f) | "Once elected … must use the de minimis rate for **all** Federal awards" until a negotiated rate is obtained. A strategic decision, not a checkbox. |
| **MTDC base is smaller than it looks** | §200.1 *MTDC* | Excludes equipment, capital expenditures, rental costs, and each subaward above $50,000 — so 15% recovers less than customers expect. |
| **DOJ FY26 content restrictions** | CTAS FY26 NOFO | New bars on funds "To promote gender ideology" or advancing "diversity, equity, and inclusion" could reach a platform's features, content, or marketing claims. |
| **Stale agency guidance** | Observed throughout | fema.gov served Jan-2023 content; epa.gov / internetforall.gov / acf.hhs.gov render with links stripped. **Re-verify any current-status claim in a live browser.** |
